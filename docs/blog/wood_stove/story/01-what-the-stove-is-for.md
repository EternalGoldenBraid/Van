# 1. What I Want From A Wood Stove

**Draft, 2026-10-21.** The stove and main chimney parts are ordered, not
installed. This chapter describes the intended use, not results from living
with it.

## A Second Kind Of Heat

The van is a Ford Transit L3H3, being converted gradually for winter-capable
living and working. The wood stove is not meant to replace the diesel heater
as the primary or overnight heat source. I want it for daytime warmth,
cooking and a visible fire.

That distinction changes the choice. I am not trying to keep a fire alive
through the night. I can accept tending small fires, and I want usable space
on top for a pot. A large window matters too: watching the fire is part of
the reason to have it.

## Small Space, Uncertain Heat Demand

A stove that feels modest in a tent can still be a lot of heat in an insulated
van. The planning target is roughly 1-2 kW of clean heat, but I do not yet
have a measured heat-loss figure for the finished van.

Nor do I have evidence that the chosen stove can burn cleanly and continuously
at that output. An advertised maximum does not answer the minimum-output
question. Reports from other vans are useful context, but a different stove,
chimney and insulation level can behave differently.

The working idea is small, brisk, intermittent fires if necessary, rather than
choking a large load down to a smoulder. Whether that is comfortable in this
van remains something to observe after a properly completed installation.

## Cooking Without An Oven

The chosen stove has no built-in oven. A flat-bottom Dutch oven on the cooktop
is the intended alternative, provided the actual top, flue position and
supported weight suit it. I have not bought a pot on the strength of a product
photo.

A small water tank was another possibility, but it would not solve excessive
heat output. Water stores heat temporarily; if left in the van, it eventually
gives that heat back.

## The Purchase Is Only Part Of The Project

A stove price is not an installation budget. The chimney, weather seal,
supports, hearth, shielding, restraints and safety equipment all matter.
Research and fabrication take time too.

The [shopping list](../shopping_list_boutique.md) keeps those distinctions visible.
It separates the amounts attached to confirmed orders from optional accessories
and items that still have no price. The full project cost is not known yet.
