# 3. The Chimney Became Its Own Project

**Draft, 2026-10-21.** The roof boot and three main chimney components are
ordered. This is the purchasing story, not a completed roof-passage design.

## Three Different Jobs At The Roof

The chimney needs to carry smoke out. The roof passage needs to keep heat
away from combustible construction. The weather seal needs to keep water out.
The pipe also needs proper structural support.

Those jobs cannot all be assigned to a silicone boot. The boot is a weather
seal, not a bracket or a firestop. Likewise, wrapping a single-wall pipe with
loose wool and metal is not automatically equivalent to a designed insulated
chimney system.

Other van builds were useful references, but required careful reading.
Vantastic's example used already-insulated double-wall pipe, with additional
wool at the passage. It was not evidence for putting bare single-wall pipe
through the roof with only a homemade wrap.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f2yzkCgORDw

## The Parts I Ordered

The chosen route is:

**Boutique's supplied single-wall flue -> DW37F080 connector ->
DW13080 insulated section -> DW1076080 terminal/rain hood.**

The Jeremias DW-FU section has an 80 mm bore and a 145 mm outer shell. A
separate Master Flash silicone #4 boot fits around that outer diameter.
Actual fit, clearances, fire protection and mounting still need resolving
in the physical installation.

The assembly is illustrated in {numref}`fig-chimney-drawio`, with an earlier
concept in {numref}`fig-chimney-excalidraw`. The
[interactive layout explorer](../layout.md) makes the height and placement
assumptions adjustable without changing either source drawing.

I chose the 1000 mm section, with 940 mm of effective installed length.
The 500 mm alternative could leave too little length to span the required
indoor transition, roof build-up and outdoor projection.

The aim is to keep the exterior chimney low. More insulated length can extend
downwards inside the van without necessarily raising the terminal. That gives
layout margin and can help retain exhaust heat; it does not prove adequate
draft or a safe final assembly. Floor-level, raised and final hearth positions
are still layout choices, not settled installation dimensions.

## The Cheaper Hat Was Not The Whole Terminal

A retrofit rain hood looked much cheaper than the integrated cap. The
comparison changed after checking the drawings: that hood did not close
and protect the insulated pipe's open annulus.

At Kamin-store24's observed prices, the separate hood and bare terminal
together cost **EUR 118.30**, against **EUR 101.60** for the integrated
terminal/hood. Making a spark screen would not remove the need for the
terminal, and a screen introduces its own restriction and cleaning questions.

The ordered Feuerdepot integrated terminal was **EUR 87.20** at checkout.
These are component comparisons, not additional purchases.

## Finland Delivery Was The Actual Test

Kamin-store24 listed a Finland delivery rate, but the real checkout reported
no shipping methods for the destination. A published country list was not
enough.

Feuerdepot had the exact three selected parts. Finland was available as a
delivery country, and my final checkout then showed **EUR 45.00
shipping**. That was much stronger evidence than a generic claim to serve
the EU.

The German free-shipping banner was a distraction: it applied to Germany,
not Finland. Their separate Finland table also had a surprising jump in
freight above EUR 300 of goods, so adding accessories was not automatically
a saving.
https://www.feuerdepot.de/ihre-versandkosten

## What Has Not Been Bought Or Proven

Ordering these parts does not finish the installation. Structural support,
road retention, the hearth, shielding, roof fire protection, adhesives/sealants
and the final attachment hardware are still open. Their costs are unknown,
not included as zero. The stove's actual paid total is also missing from the record.

The [floor work plan](../../floor/index.md) sets out the current floor work and
preparation for stove installation. Measurements and actual construction
evidence will follow; there is no installation-success or performance claim
to write yet.
