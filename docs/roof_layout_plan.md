# Roof Layout Plan

Status: layout options for the Ford Transit L3H3. Solar installation can wait,
but the fan location must remain compatible with each option.

## Known Dimensions

The [Transit dimension reference](https://vanreviewer.co.uk/ford/transit/dimensions/1801/)
gives the L3H3 as:

- 3,750 mm wheelbase
- 3,494 mm interior cargo length
- 2,059 mm body width
- 2,780 mm overall height

These dimensions confirm the van variant but not the usable roof rectangle.
Before cutting or ordering panels, measure:

- flat roof length behind the front slope
- usable flat width at the panel mounting height
- roof-rib and OEM rack-point centres
- the fan's cutout, flange, closed height, and open-lid envelope
- clearances required by the fan and panel mounting systems

## Common Fan Position

Lock the fan to the **foremost sufficiently flat roof bay behind the front
curved transition**, outside structural ribs. This longitudinal position keeps
the long roof rectangle behind it available for either panels or cargo.

Do not yet lock the lateral position. When the fan or its exact template is
available, move it toward the passenger side only as far as the flange or a
manufacturer-approved shaped adapter remains fully supported. An outboard
position is preferable because it can leave a longitudinal cargo lane beside
the fan; a centred position is the fallback if the roof crown prevents this.

Do not place the fan on the front curved slope merely to avoid the rack. The
exact cut remains blocked on the roof and fan measurements.

A canoe or skis may pass above the closed fan on sufficiently high crossbars,
but the fan cannot open while cargo obstructs its lid. Verify the rack's dynamic
load rating and the van's VIN-specific roof-load limit before combining panels,
rack, and cargo.

This is not a choice between solar forever and cargo forever. A rack can later
either preserve a low lane beside the fan or carry long cargo above the closed
fan and panels. The latter costs height, access, and some solar output from
shading while loaded.

## Future Chimney

The wood stove and chimney are deferred and do not block this year's ceiling
insulation. Do not leave a second roof area uninsulated for an undecided stove.

For future compatibility:

- show a provisional vertical flue corridor on the roof layout
- keep that corridor clear of hidden wiring and permanent ceiling furniture
- use a removable ceiling panel there if practical
- do not cut or reserve an exact hole until the stove, shielded clearances,
  flue diameter, flashing, and interior position are confirmed
- include the final chimney exclusion zone when selecting the solar array

The useful terms are **longitudinal** for front-to-back placement and
**lateral** or **transverse** for side-to-side placement. The chimney's lateral
offset does not need to put it completely outside the rack. A modular rack can
have a dedicated open bay around a nearly vertical flue. Its crossbars, deck,
panels, and cargo must remain outside the stove/flue manufacturer's hot
clearance; a flue guard does not reduce that required clearance.

Do not add flue bends merely to clear a rack rail. Choose the stove's interior
position first, project the preferred vertical flue centreline onto the roof,
and then place or omit rack members around its exclusion zone. Also check
separation from the fan and opening windows.

An exhaust-mode roof fan can depressurize the van and reverse the stove's
chimney draft; the fan does not need to be blowing inward to pull smoke or
carbon monoxide inside. Unless the completed installation has a designed
combustion-air supply and has been tested for worst-case depressurization, do
not operate the stove and extraction fan together. The stove installation must
include approved carbon-monoxide and smoke alarms.

The final flue height and whether its upper section is removed for driving must
follow the selected system's instructions.

The fan remains the only roof opening that must be reserved now.

must be physically templated.

## Layout A: Cargo First

- Preserve a roughly 750-900 mm full-length central lane for a canoe or skis.
- Put the fan at the front of one equipment strip.
- Put two approximately 175 W narrow panels end-to-end behind it along the same
  roof edge.
- Nominal panel envelope: 2,970 x 668 mm, before mounting gaps.
- **Array target: approximately 350 W.**

This is the easiest layout for long roof cargo. It also leaves the clearest
service access, but solar will not cover the modeled daily electrical use
outside strong summer conditions.

## Layout B: Balanced

- Start with the two-panel strip from Layout A.
- Add one approximately 175 W panel on the opposite side where the fan,
  curvature, and rack points allow.
- **Array target: approximately 525 W.**

The third panel interrupts an unobstructed cargo lane at panel height. Retain
crossbars so long cargo can travel above that panel, or remove the third panel
from the final design if a low full-length lane is essential.

## Layout C: Full Solar

Check both packings against the measured roof:

1. Four narrow panels as transverse rows: approximately 2,672 x 1,485 mm,
   **700 W**, plus a separate fan bay.
2. Two 410 W panels across the roof: approximately 2,268 x 1,724 mm,
   **820 W**, plus a separate fan bay.

The 820 W option uses less roof length but its 1,724 mm panel width may exceed
the Transit H3's usable rack width. The 700 W option is narrower but needs more
roof length once the fan and service gaps are included. Neither packing is
approved until physically templated.

Full solar largely gives up an unobstructed same-height cargo lane. Canoe or ski
carrying would require a rack above the array, with added height, wind loading,
shade, and access constraints.

## Charging Comparison

Current planning references:

- battery bank: 400 Ah at 12.8 V = 5.12 kWh nominal
- modeled daily loads: approximately 1.85 kWh
- alternator charging: 30 A = 384 W ideal, or a possible future 50 A = 640 W

Conservative good-day production below assumes four peak-sun-hours and 20%
system loss:

| Layout | Array | Peak charge at 14.4 V | Good-day energy | 5.12 kWh refill from empty |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cargo first | 350 W | about 24 A | 1.12 kWh | 4.6 good days |
| Balanced | 525 W | about 36 A | 1.68 kWh | 3.0 good days |
| Full, narrow panels | 700 W | about 49 A | 2.24 kWh | 2.3 good days |
| Full, large panels | 820 W | about 57 A | 2.62 kWh | 2.0 good days |

Charge current is an array-level estimate. The electrical plan must select an
MPPT and series/parallel arrangement from the final panels' Voc, Isc, and cold
weather limits.

### Finland Seasonal Output

[PVGIS](https://re.jrc.ec.europa.eu/pvg_tools/en/) estimates for a flat,
unshaded crystalline-silicon array in Helsinki, including 14% system losses:

| Layout | June average | September average | October average | November average |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 350 W | 1.64 kWh/day | 0.68 kWh/day | 0.27 kWh/day | 0.06 kWh/day |
| 525 W | 2.46 kWh/day | 1.02 kWh/day | 0.41 kWh/day | 0.09 kWh/day |
| 700 W | 3.28 kWh/day | 1.36 kWh/day | 0.55 kWh/day | 0.13 kWh/day |
| 820 W | 3.85 kWh/day | 1.59 kWh/day | 0.64 kWh/day | 0.15 kWh/day |

This supports delaying the panel purchase: by October and November, even the
full array contributes only a minority of the modeled 1.85 kWh daily use.
Shading, snow, dirt, and parking orientation can reduce output further.

### Solar Versus Driving

Compared with the 820 W layout on a four-peak-sun-hour day:

| Layout | Solar given up | Equivalent 30 A driving | Equivalent 50 A driving |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cargo first, 350 W | 1.50 kWh/day | 3.9 h | 2.4 h |
| Balanced, 525 W | 0.94 kWh/day | 2.5 h | 1.5 h |
| Full narrow, 700 W | 0.38 kWh/day | 1.0 h | 0.6 h |

These driving figures are idealized and exclude conversion losses and charge
taper. They show the scale of the compromise: regular driving can compensate
for the cargo-first layout, but not with only a short daily trip.

## Decision Sequence

### Current build phase

1. Mark the foremost flat roof bay as the fan stripe.
2. Insulate the rest of the cargo ceiling.
3. Install the fan after checking its physical flange/template, ribs, and roof
   curvature.
4. Complete the diesel-heater and electrical work.

### Deferred roof phase

1. Measure and photograph the usable roof, ribs, seams, and rack points.
2. Make cardboard rectangles for the fan, provisional chimney corridor,
   1,485 x 668 mm panels, and
   1,724 x 1,134 mm panels.
3. Test all three layouts with the fan, chimney corridor, and cargo lane
   marked.
4. Select the stove and fix its interior position before locking the chimney
   penetration.
5. Design rack rails and crossbars around the chimney's required hot-clearance
   zone.
6. Select the final panels, rack, and MPPT when solar installation is timely.
